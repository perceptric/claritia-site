## What is an inbound lead?

An inbound lead is a person or company that contacts your business first, after finding you on their own. They might fill in a demo request form, book a call, start a chat, download a guide or sign up for a trial. Because they chose to reach out, they already have some interest in what you sell.

The opposite is an outbound lead, someone your team found and contacted first through cold email, calls or social messages. We compare the two in detail in [inbound vs outbound leads](/blog/inbound-vs-outbound-leads).

## How inbound leads work

1. **They find you.** Through search, an AI answer, a blog post, a referral, a review site or an ad.
2. **They take an action.** They fill in a form, start a chat, book a time or sign up.
3. **You capture their details.** Name, email and a few answers, plus the page and campaign they came from. See [what lead capture is](/blog/what-is-lead-capture).
4. **You qualify them.** Their answers tell you if they fit and what they need. Our guide to [lead qualification](/blog/lead-qualification) covers how.
5. **You route them.** Each lead goes to the right person, by territory, deal size, product or whose turn it is.
6. **They book a meeting.** Ideally on the spot, with the right person, before they leave your site.
7. **You follow up.** Reminders before the call, and a nudge if they didn't book or didn't show.

## Examples of inbound leads

### High intent

These leads are asking to talk to you. Treat them as the most urgent.

- **Demo requests.** A buyer wants to see the product.
- **Contact sales forms.** A buyer has a question about price or fit.
- **Quote requests.** A buyer wants a number for a specific job.
- **Booked calls.** A visitor picked a time on your booking page.
- **Pricing page chats.** A visitor asks a question while looking at plans.

### Lower intent

These leads showed interest but are earlier in their search. They often need a follow-up before they are ready to talk.

- **Content downloads.** A guide, a report or a template.
- **Newsletter signups.** They like what you write.
- **Webinar signups.** They want to learn about the topic.
- **Free trial or free plan signups.** They want to try the product on their own first.

### Referrals

A referral is usually counted as inbound when the lead reaches out to you after someone recommends you. They tend to come in warm, with some trust already in place.

## Inbound lead vs MQL and SQL

Many teams sort inbound leads into stages:

- **Inbound lead:** Anyone who reached out.
- **Marketing qualified lead (MQL):** A lead that fits your target buyer and showed enough interest for more attention, such as a content download from the right kind of company.
- **Sales qualified lead (SQL):** A lead that sales has checked and wants to talk to, such as a demo request with a real need and budget.

A demo request from a good fit can skip straight to sales. A newsletter signup usually needs more time.

## Best practices for inbound leads

### Respond fast

The person who just filled in your form is thinking about the problem right now. A reply the next day reaches someone who has moved on. The fastest response is no wait at all: let them book a meeting inside the form.

*In Claritia, the thank-you screen opens the right rep's calendar as soon as the form is sent.*

### Send each lead to one owner

A lead in a shared inbox belongs to no one. Use rules to give each lead an owner the moment it arrives. If the person is already in your CRM, send them to the rep who owns the account.

### Ask a few useful questions

Ask for what you need to route and prepare: company size, what they need and when. Skip the rest. Each extra field costs you leads.

### Keep the source

Save the page, the referrer and the UTM tags with each lead. Over time, this shows which channels bring in your best inbound leads.

### Have a path for every lead

Not every inbound lead is ready for a sales call. Send small companies or students to a guide or a self-serve plan, and save rep time for the leads who fit.

### Follow up until they book

Some leads fill in the form and never pick a time. A follow-up the next day, with the booking link, gives them an easy second chance.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating every lead the same.** A demo request and a newsletter signup need different next steps.
- **A shared inbox with no owner.** Everyone assumes someone else will reply.
- **Round robin with no checks.** A lead goes to a rep who is on holiday.
- **Losing track of returning leads.** A known customer is treated like a stranger.
- **Asking for a meeting by email.** A booking link sent hours later gets fewer replies than a calendar shown right after the form.

## Questions

### What is an inbound lead in sales?

An inbound lead in sales is a potential buyer who contacted your company first, such as by filling in a demo request or contact form. Sales teams usually treat them as warmer than outbound leads, since the buyer already has a reason to talk.

### What is an example of an inbound lead?

A marketing manager reads your blog post, visits your pricing page and fills in your demo request form. That person is an inbound lead. So is someone who downloads your guide or starts a chat on your site.

### Are inbound leads better than outbound leads?

Inbound leads are usually warmer and easier to book, because they came to you. Outbound gives you more control over who you reach. Most B2B teams use both. See [inbound vs outbound leads](/blog/inbound-vs-outbound-leads) for the full comparison.

### How quickly should you respond to an inbound lead?

As fast as you can, ideally within minutes. The surest way is to let the lead book a meeting inside the form, so there is no wait at all.

### Is a referral an inbound lead?

Usually, yes. When a referred person reaches out to you on their own, most teams count them as inbound.

### How do you get more inbound leads?

Be easy to find and easy to contact. Publish useful content, show up in search and AI answers, ask happy customers for referrals, and make the form short. Our guide to [inbound lead generation](/blog/inbound-lead-generation) covers each step.
