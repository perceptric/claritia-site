Small B2B teams hear two kinds of advice. One camp says to write content and let buyers come to you. The other says to pick your dream accounts and reach out. Both are right some of the time, and it's hard to plan when the terms get used loosely.

The difference between inbound and outbound leads comes down to who makes the first move. That one fact changes how warm the lead is, how fast you need to act and what your team spends its time on.

## Main points

- An inbound lead reached out to you first. An outbound lead is someone your team contacted first.
- Inbound leads are usually warmer and easier to book. Outbound lets you choose exactly who you talk to.
- Inbound work is about speed and routing. Outbound work is about lists, research and many follow-ups.

## What is the difference between inbound and outbound leads?

An inbound lead is a person who contacts your business first, for example by filling in a form, booking a call or starting a chat.

An outbound lead is a person your team contacts first, through cold email, calls, social messages or events. Inbound leads start with the buyer's interest, and outbound leads start with your choice of who to reach.

For a closer look at the first kind, read [what an inbound lead is](/blog/what-is-an-inbound-lead).

## Inbound vs outbound leads at a glance

| | Inbound leads | Outbound leads |
|---|---|---|
| Who reaches out first | The buyer | Your team |
| Typical sources | Search, AI answers, content, referrals, review sites, ads | Cold email, calls, LinkedIn messages, events |
| How warm they are | Usually warm, with a reason to talk | Usually cold at first |
| Who you reach | Whoever finds you | The accounts you pick |
| What matters most | Speed, routing and an easy way to book | A good list, research and steady follow-up |
| Main cost | Time to build content and a site that converts | Rep time, data and sending tools |
| How fast it starts | Slow to build, then steady | Fast to start, stops when you stop |
| Common risk | Leads wait too long or go to the wrong person | Low reply rates and a bad sender reputation |

## How inbound leads work

1. **A buyer has a problem.** They search, ask an AI tool, or ask a peer.
2. **They find you.** Through a blog post, a review site, a referral or an ad.
3. **They reach out.** They fill in a form, book a time or start a chat.
4. **You qualify and route them.** Their answers decide who they talk to.
5. **They book a meeting.** Ideally right after the form, before they leave your site.

The work happens before and after the lead arrives. Before, you make your business easy to find. After, you make sure no lead waits. Our guide to [inbound lead generation](/blog/inbound-lead-generation) covers both.

## How outbound leads work

1. **You choose who to reach.** A list of companies and roles that fit your buyer.
2. **You research them.** Their needs, their tools and a reason to reach out now.
3. **You make contact.** A cold email, a call or a social message.
4. **You follow up.** Several touches over days or weeks, since most people don't reply to the first.
5. **They reply or book.** Now they become an active conversation, and the next steps look much like inbound.

## Examples

### Inbound lead examples

- An operations lead reads your blog post on a problem they have, then fills in your demo request form.
- A founder asks a peer for a tool, gets your name and books a call on your site.
- A buyer finds you on a review site and starts a chat on your pricing page.
- A marketer downloads your guide and later replies to your follow-up email.

### Outbound lead examples

- A rep emails heads of sales at 50 companies that just raised money, and three reply.
- A founder sends a short LinkedIn message to a buyer they met at an event.
- An SDR calls a list of companies that use a tool your product connects to.

## Pros and cons

### Inbound leads

**Pros:**

- Buyers come with a reason to talk, so conversations start warmer.
- Good content keeps bringing leads after you publish it.
- Your team spends time on people who already showed interest.

**Cons:**

- You can't choose who finds you, so some leads won't fit.
- It takes time before content and search bring steady leads.
- Leads go cold fast if nobody responds right away.

### Outbound leads

**Pros:**

- You choose the exact companies and roles you want.
- It can start this week, without waiting for content to rank.
- It works for new markets where buyers don't know to look for you.

**Cons:**

- Most people don't reply, so it takes many touches to get a few talks.
- It uses a lot of rep time.
- Sending too much cold email can hurt your domain's reputation.

## Which should a small B2B team focus on?

It depends on your stage and your buyers.

- **If buyers already search for what you sell**, inbound usually gives the best return. Put your effort into content, a short form and fast booking.
- **If you sell something new**, or to a short list of large accounts, outbound gets you in front of them sooner.
- **If you're just starting**, a little outbound gets early conversations while you build inbound.

Most teams end up with both. The real gain often comes from handling inbound well, because those leads are already raising a hand. A team that books every good inbound lead on the spot gets more from the traffic it already has.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating inbound leads like outbound ones.** Sending a warm demo request into a slow, multi-week sequence.
- **No owner for inbound leads.** They sit in a shared inbox while reps focus on outbound targets.
- **Mixing the numbers.** Inbound and outbound convert at different rates, so track them apart.
- **Losing the source.** Without UTM tags and a source field, you can't tell which leads came from where.
- **Cold email from your main domain at high volume.** Many teams send outbound from a separate domain to protect their main one.

## Questions

### What is an inbound lead?

An inbound lead is someone who contacted your business first, for example by filling in a form, booking a call or starting a chat on your site.

### What is an outbound lead?

An outbound lead is someone your team contacted first, through cold email, a call, a social message or an event, before they showed any interest.

### Which converts better, inbound or outbound?

Inbound leads usually convert at a higher rate, because the buyer chose to reach out. Outbound gives you more control over who you reach. Track both apart to see what holds true for your team.

### Is a referral inbound or outbound?

Most teams count a referral as inbound when the referred person reaches out to you on their own. If your team contacts someone after a referral, some teams count it as outbound.

### Is social media inbound or outbound?

Both. A buyer who sees your post and visits your site is inbound. A message your rep sends to someone who never engaged with you is outbound.

### Can a small team do both inbound and outbound?

Yes. Many small teams use outbound to start conversations and inbound to keep a steady flow. Keep the two in separate lists so you can see what each brings in.
