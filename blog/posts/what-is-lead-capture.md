## What is lead capture?

Lead capture is the process of collecting contact details from a website visitor, usually a name and an email, in exchange for something they want. That could be a demo, a quote, a guide or an answer to a question. The captured details become a lead your team can follow up with.

Lead capture is one part of lead generation. Lead generation covers everything that brings people to you, like search, ads and referrals. Lead capture is the moment one of those people raises a hand and tells you who they are.

## What is a lead capture form?

A lead capture form is a web form built to turn a visitor into a lead. It asks for contact details and often a few qualifying questions, such as company size or what the person needs. When they submit it, the answers are saved and sent to your CRM, your inbox or your team chat.

A good lead capture form is short, asks only what you will use, and ends with a clear next step. On a sales site, that next step is often a booked call. ## How lead capture works

1. **A visitor arrives.** They find you through search, an ad, a post or a referral.
2. **You make an offer.** A demo, a quote, a free guide, a price estimate or a quick answer in chat.
3. **They share their details.** They fill in a form, a popup or a chat with their name, email and a few answers.
4. **The details are saved with context.** Good tools keep the page, the referrer and the UTM tags with each lead, so you know where it came from.
5. **The lead goes somewhere useful.** Your CRM, a sales rep, an email list or a Slack channel.
6. **Someone follows up.** Ideally right away, with a booked meeting or a reply while the lead still remembers you.

Steps 5 and 6 are where most leads are lost. A form that only sends an email to a shared inbox depends on someone reading it in time.

## Types of lead capture

Most websites use more than one of these. Each fits a different moment in a visit.

- **Embedded forms.** A form inside the page, like a demo request or contact form. It suits visitors who came ready to act.
- **Popups and slide-ins.** A form that appears after a delay, on scroll or when someone is about to leave. It suits content pages where people read but don't act.
- **Live chat and chatbots.** A chat window that asks a few questions and collects an email. It suits visitors with a question before they commit.
- **Landing pages.** A page built around one offer and one form, often for paid ads.
- **Booking pages.** A calendar where the visitor picks a time and leaves their details. It suits leads who already know they want to talk.
- **Gated content.** A guide, report or template that visitors get in return for their email.

## Best practices for lead capture

### Ask for less

Each extra field is one more reason to leave. Ask for what you need to take the next step and nothing more. If some questions only matter for certain leads, use conditional logic to show them only when an earlier answer calls for it.

### Keep the source

Add hidden fields for UTM tags, the page and the referrer. Without them, you can't tell which campaign brought in your best leads.

### Give a next step on the spot

Don't end on a plain thank-you message if you can avoid it. Show a calendar, send the guide, or tell the lead exactly when they will hear back. A form that books the meeting right after submit saves a round of emails.

### Send each lead to the right place

A lead that sits in a shared inbox waits. Send it to your CRM, alert the rep in Slack, or route it to the right person based on the answers. Our guide to [lead qualification](/blog/lead-qualification) covers which answers to route on.

### Keep spam out

Bots fill in public forms. Use spam protection, a honeypot field and disposable email blocking so your team only sees real people.

### Respect people's time and data

Say what you will do with their details, and don't add them to a newsletter they didn't ask for.

## Common lead capture mistakes

- **Too many fields up front.** A long form on the first visit scares people off.
- **No follow-up plan.** Leads come in, and nobody owns them.
- **One form for every visitor.** A student, a partner and a buyer all get the same path.
- **Lost campaign data.** Leads arrive with no record of where they came from.
- **Popups that fire too soon.** A popup in the first second, before anyone has read a line, feels like a wall.

## Questions

### What is the difference between lead capture and lead generation?

Lead generation is everything that brings people to your business, like content, ads and referrals. Lead capture is the step where a visitor shares their contact details, usually through a form, so they become a lead you can follow up with.

### What should a lead capture form include?

A name, an email and one to three questions that help you take the next step, such as company size or what the person needs. Add hidden fields for the campaign source, and end with a clear next step like a booked call.

### Where should I put lead capture forms on my website?

Put a form on every page where a visitor is likely to act: the home page, pricing, product pages and a contact page. Use a popup or slide-in on blog posts, where people read but rarely look for a form.

### Do popups work for lead capture?

They can, when they show at a sensible time and offer something useful. A popup that appears as someone reaches the end of a post, or is about to leave, feels less pushy than one that appears the moment the page loads.

### What is a lead capture page?

A lead capture page is a landing page built around one offer and one form. It has little else on it, so the visitor's only real choice is to fill in the form or leave. Teams often use them for paid ads.

### How fast should I follow up with a captured lead?

As fast as you can. A lead that just filled in your form is thinking about the problem right now. Booking the meeting inside the form is the quickest follow-up there is. Read more in our guide to [inbound lead generation](/blog/inbound-lead-generation).
