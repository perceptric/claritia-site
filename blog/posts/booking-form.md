## What is a booking form?

A booking form is an online form where a customer reserves a time, a date or an item with your business. It collects their contact details, what they want and when, then confirms the booking or sends it to you to approve. Common types include appointments, consultations, classes, events, rentals and service visits.

The best booking forms connect to your calendar. They only show times you're free, add the booking to your calendar, and send the customer a confirmation and reminders.

## Booking form vs booking page

The two overlap, but they start in a different place.

| | Booking form | Booking page |
|---|---|---|
| What comes first | Questions | Open times |
| Best for | Bookings that depend on details, like the service, size or location | Simple meetings anyone can book |
| Who gets the booking | Can depend on the answers | Usually one person or a team |
| Examples | Plumbing visit, car rental, consultation with intake questions | Intro call, office hours |

A booking page is fine when every booking is the same. A booking form is better when you need to know what the job is before you pick the time or the person. Many tools now do both: the form asks the questions, and its last page is the calendar.

## Fields to include in a booking form

Start with the fields every booking needs:

- **Name.** To greet them and fill the calendar invite.
- **Email.** For the confirmation, reminders and any changes.
- **The service or item.** As a list to pick from, so you don't get free text you have to decode.
- **A time, date or date range.** A calendar for appointments, a date range for rentals, a session list for classes and events.

Then add only the fields that change the job:

- **Phone,** when you call or text before the visit.
- **Address,** for visits at the customer's home or site.
- **Party or group size,** for tables, events and rentals.
- **New or returning customer,** when a first visit takes longer.
- **A short note or photo,** when it helps you prepare, like a photo of a leak.
- **Payment or deposit,** when no-shows cost you money.
- **Consent or a waiver,** for activities that need one.

Keep the campaign source in hidden fields, so you know which ad or post brought the booking.

## 8 booking form examples

Each example has a line on when to use it, the fields to ask, why it works and free templates to start from.

### 1. Appointment booking form

**When to use it:** Clinics, salons, pet groomers and any business that books people into time slots.

**Fields:**

- Name, phone and email
- New or returning
- Reason for visit, as a list
- Preferred date
- Morning, afternoon or evening

**Why it works:** The reason for visit tells you how long to book. New or returning flags who needs extra time for paperwork.

### 2. Consultation booking form

**When to use it:** Coaches, consultants, lawyers, accountants and designers who start with a first call.

**Fields:**

- Name
- Email
- What do you want to work on?
- Pick a time

**Why it works:** One open question gives you enough to prepare without making the first step feel like homework. The calendar comes right after, so the call is booked before they leave.

### 3. Event booking form

**When to use it:** Conferences, workshops, meetups and dinners where people book a seat.

**Fields:**

- Name and email
- Company and job title, for business events
- Ticket type, such as general, VIP or student
- Dietary needs
- Agreement to the code of conduct

**Why it works:** Ticket type sorts guests, and dietary needs saves a round of emails before the day. A required code of conduct box sets the tone.

### 4. Rental booking form

**When to use it:** Car, equipment, party and vacation rentals.

**Fields:**

- Pick-up location
- Pick-up and return dates
- The item, such as the car type
- Extras, such as a child seat or an extra driver
- Name, email and phone
- A driver's license or ID upload, when you need one

**Why it works:** Dates and the item come first, so the customer sees whether it's free before typing their details. Prices on each option, with a live total, answer the first question every renter has.

### 5. Class booking form

**When to use it:** Gyms, yoga studios, music teachers, tutors and cooking schools.

**Fields:**

- Name, email and phone
- Which class
- Preferred day, such as weekday morning, weekday evening or weekend
- Experience level

**Why it works:** Experience level puts a beginner in the right class. A trial class form like this turns a curious visitor into a first booking.

Start from the [class and lesson templates](/templates/category/class).

### 6. Service visit booking form

**When to use it:** Plumbers, electricians, HVAC techs, cleaners and anyone who works at the customer's home.

**Fields:**

- What's the problem, as a list
- Which room or area
- How urgent it is
- Photos
- Name, email and phone
- Service address
- Pick a time

**Why it works:** The problem and urgency come first, so an emergency doesn't get booked like a tune-up. Photos help the tech bring the right parts.

Start from the [home service booking templates](/templates/category/booking/construction).

### 7. Reservation form

**When to use it:** Restaurants, hotels, spas and private dining.

**Fields:**

- Date and time
- Party size
- Name, phone and email
- Special requests

**Why it works:** It's short, because people book tables on their phones. Special requests catch birthdays and allergies before the night.

Start from the [reservation form templates](/templates/category/reservation).

### 8. Booking request form

**When to use it:** When you confirm each booking by hand, such as a venue, a custom job or a busy calendar you manage yourself.

**Fields:**

- Name and email
- The day they want
- A time window, such as 8 to 10 am or noon to 2 pm
- What it's for

**Why it works:** The customer gives you a window, not an exact slot, and you confirm the time. It keeps you in control while still saving the back and forth.

## How to put a booking form on your website

1. **Pick a template or describe the form.** Start from one of the examples above, then cut every field you won't use.
2. **Connect your calendar.** Link Google Calendar or Outlook, so only free times show and each booking lands on your calendar.
3. **Set your rules.** Working hours, buffers between bookings, how much notice you need and a daily cap.
4. **Add the booking step.** Make the calendar the last page of the form, or send people to it from the thank-you screen.
5. **Turn on reminders.** A confirmation with the invite, then reminders a day and an hour before.
6. **Put it on your site.** Share the link, add a QR code, or paste one line of code into your page. Claritia has guides for [WordPress](/integrations/wordpress), [Webflow](/integrations/webflow) and [Squarespace](/integrations/squarespace).
7. **Test it.** Book a slot yourself, on a phone and a laptop, and check the invite, the reminder and the reschedule link.

## Common mistakes

- **Asking everything up front.** Long forms lose people. Ask the rest at the visit or in a follow-up.
- **No calendar connection.** Customers pick times you're busy, and you spend the day rescheduling.
- **Free text for the service.** "The usual" doesn't tell you how long to book. Use a list.
- **No reminders.** A booking a week out is easy to forget.
- **No way to reschedule.** Without a link, people cancel or don't show up.
