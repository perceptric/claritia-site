What Is Lead Qualification? BANT, Other Frameworks and Form Questions (2026)

What is lead qualification? Learn how sales lead qualification works, how BANT and other frameworks help, and the form questions that qualify before booking.

Your reps have a limited number of calls in a week. When half of them go to people who can't buy, don't need what you sell or are just curious, the good leads wait. Some of them never get a call at all.

Lead qualification is how you decide who is worth a sales conversation, and when. Done by hand, it means a rep reads every form and makes a guess. Done well, the form asks the right questions and sends each lead down the right path on its own.

Main points

  • Lead qualification checks whether a lead fits your product and is ready to talk, so reps spend time on the right people.

What is lead qualification?

Lead qualification is the process of deciding whether a lead is a good fit for your product and ready for a sales conversation. Teams check things like company size, need, budget, authority and timing, often with a framework such as BANT. Qualified leads go to sales, and the rest get a different next step.

In sales, a lead that passes is often called a sales qualified lead (SQL). A lead that fits your target buyer but isn't ready to talk yet is often called a marketing qualified lead (MQL).

How lead qualification works

  1. Set your criteria. Write down what a good-fit lead looks like: company size, industry, role, need and anything that rules a lead out.
  2. Ask the questions. On the form, in chat, or on a first call.
  3. Sort the answers. Good fit, maybe, or not a fit. Some teams add points to get a score.
  4. Pick the next step. Book a sales call, send a guide, offer a self-serve plan or follow up later.
  5. Route to the right person. By territory, deal size, product or whose turn it is.
  6. Review and adjust. Check which leads turned into deals and change the criteria if needed.

BANT

BANT is one of the oldest and best-known qualification frameworks. It asks four things:

  • Budget. Can the lead pay for what you sell?
  • Authority. Is this person the one who decides, or close to them?
  • Need. Do they have a problem your product solves?
  • Timeline. When do they plan to buy or start?

BANT is simple to learn and quick to use, which is why it's still common. Its weak spot is that buyers often don't know their budget yet, and asking about it first can feel pushy. Many teams now start with need and timeline, and leave budget for later or ask it as a rough range.

Other qualification frameworks

CHAMP

CHAMP reorders the same ideas to put the buyer's problem first:

  • Challenges. What problem are they trying to solve?
  • Authority. Who decides?
  • Money. Is there budget, or a way to get it?
  • Prioritization. How urgent is this compared with their other work?

It suits teams that sell by solving a clear pain and want the first conversation to feel helpful.

MEDDIC

MEDDIC is used for larger, more complex B2B deals with several people involved:

  • Metrics. What result does the buyer want, in numbers?
  • Economic buyer. Who controls the budget?
  • Decision criteria. How will they judge the options?
  • Decision process. What steps happen before they sign?
  • Identify pain. What problem is driving the purchase?
  • Champion. Who inside the company is pushing for you?

MEDDIC is too much for a web form. It fits later sales calls, after a lead has already passed a first check.

Which framework should you use?

For most small B2B teams, a light version of BANT or CHAMP on the form is enough. Use the form to answer two questions: does this lead fit, and how soon do they need help? Leave deeper questions for the first call.

Lead qualification questions for a form

Pick two to four of these. Each one should change what happens next. If an answer wouldn't change the route or help the rep prepare, drop the question.

Tips for asking them well:

  • Use choices instead of open text. "2 to 10, 11 to 50, 51 or more" is faster to answer and easier to route on.
  • Ask budget as a range, or not at all. A wide range feels safer to share.
  • Use a work email. Block disposable email addresses to keep out fake leads.
  • Ask more only when needed. Use conditional logic to show a follow-up question only for some answers.

How a form can qualify before booking

The best time to qualify is inside the form, before anyone books. Here's how it works:

  1. The lead answers a few questions. Team size, need and timeline, for example.
  2. Rules read the answers. For example: if team size is 51 or more, book the enterprise demo. If the country is Germany, book with the rep who covers it. If the budget is under your floor, send a starter guide.
  3. Good-fit leads see a calendar. The right person's open times show right after submit, with the lead's name and email filled in.
  4. Other leads get a useful next step. A guide, a self-serve plan or a shorter intro call.
  5. Your team sees why. A log shows which rule matched for each lead, so you can fix a rule when it sends someone to the wrong place.

Common mistakes

  • Too many questions. A long qualifying form loses good leads along with the bad ones.
  • Asking budget first. It can scare off buyers who haven't set one yet.
  • Questions that change nothing. If no route uses the answer, it's only friction.
  • No path for "not now." A lead who isn't ready today may be next quarter. Give them something useful.
  • Never checking the criteria. Compare qualified leads with closed deals, and adjust.
  • Rules nobody can read. If no one knows why a lead went somewhere, mistakes go unnoticed.

Questions

What is lead qualification in sales?

Lead qualification in sales is deciding whether a lead fits your product and is ready to talk. Sales teams use it to spend their time on buyers who are likely to buy, and to give other leads a different next step.

What does BANT stand for?

BANT stands for budget, authority, need and timeline. It's a simple checklist for whether a lead can buy, can decide, needs what you sell and plans to act soon.

What is the difference between an MQL and an SQL?

A marketing qualified lead (MQL) fits your target buyer and has shown some interest, such as downloading a guide. A sales qualified lead (SQL) has been checked and is ready for a sales conversation, such as a demo request from a good-fit company.

What questions should I ask to qualify a lead?

Ask about their team size, what they want to solve, their role and when they want to start. Add a budget range only if price often rules leads out. Keep it to two to four questions on a form.

Can you qualify leads automatically?

Yes. A form can route each lead by its answers, so good fits book a meeting and others get a guide or a self-serve plan. Claritia does this on Pro with rules you set, plus a log of every decision.

What is lead scoring?

Lead scoring gives each lead points for things like company size, role or actions on your site, then uses the total to decide the next step. It's one way to qualify leads, often used alongside a framework like BANT.
