Microsoft Teams Meeting Scheduler: How to Let People Book Teams Calls (2026)

Set up a Microsoft Teams meeting scheduler so people book Teams calls from a link, with Microsoft Bookings, Outlook Scheduling Poll or a booking tool.

Scheduling a Teams call with someone outside your company usually means an email chain. You offer a few times, they pick one that just got taken, and someone has to copy the Teams link into a new invite. Multiply that by every client, candidate or lead, and it eats hours each week.

A Teams meeting scheduler turns that into a link. The other person opens it, sees only the times you're free in Outlook, picks one, and both of you get an invite with a Teams link inside. Microsoft has its own ways to do this, and booking tools that connect to Outlook can do it too.

In this guide, we show three ways to let people book Teams meetings from a link, step by step, plus fixes for the problems people hit most.

Main points

  • Microsoft Bookings has a personal booking page (Bookings with me) and shared pages for teams. Both can add a unique Teams link to each booking.
  • Outlook's Scheduling Poll is for finding one time with a group. It lets people vote, then you send the final invite.
  • A booking tool like Claritia connects to Outlook, reads your busy times and adds a Teams link, and can put the calendar inside a form.
  • Teams links need a Microsoft 365 work or school account. Personal Outlook.com accounts can't create them in most tools.
  • Other options: Calendly, Zoho Bookings and YouCanBookMe also add Teams links.

What you need

Before you start, check these four things:

  • A Microsoft 365 work or school account. Teams meeting links come from it.
  • Outlook on the web turned on. Microsoft says Bookings needs Outlook on the web enabled.
  • A plan that includes Bookings, if you go the Microsoft route. Microsoft lists Business Basic, Business Standard and Business Premium, plus E, F and A plans and Teams Essentials and Teams Premium.
  • Your working hours set in Outlook. Every route reads your calendar to decide which times to show.

If you only need a link for your own calls, start with Step 2. If several people share the calls, go to Step 3. If the meeting starts from your website, Step 4 is the better fit.

Step 2: Set up a personal page with Bookings with me

Bookings with me, also called Personal Bookings, gives you a one-on-one booking page tied to your Outlook calendar.

  1. Go to book.ms and sign in, or add the Bookings app to the left rail in Outlook or Teams.
  2. Find the Personal booking page section. Microsoft publishes it for you with two meeting types, 15 and 30 minutes.
  3. Open a meeting type, or select Create meeting type. Give it a name, a description and a length.
  4. Set the mode to online, so each booking gets a Teams link.
  5. Choose public or private. Public types show on your page. Private types are shared by their own link, and can make single-use links.
  6. Set availability. Regular hours read your Outlook calendar, or you can set custom hours for each day. Add buffers and lead times if you need them.
  7. Copy your page link and share it by email or in your signature.

People who book don't need a Microsoft account. Each booking lands on your Outlook calendar, and Bookings can send email reminders to both of you.

Step 3: Set up a shared page for your team

Shared Bookings is for a team that takes calls together, like sales, support or advising.

  1. On the Bookings home page, go to Shared booking pages and create a page.
  2. Under Staff, add team members and pick a role for each. Leave Events on Microsoft 365 calendar affect availability on, so busy times block bookings. Microsoft turns it on by default.
  3. Under Services, select Add new service. Set a name, length and buffer time.
  4. Turn on Add online meeting. Each booking then gets a unique Teams link on both calendars and in every confirmation and reminder email.
  5. Assign the staff who can take each service.
  6. Under Booking page, set your colors, time zone and who can book. You can require a Microsoft 365 account from your company, or leave it open to anyone.
  7. Publish the page, then share the link or embed it on your site.

Microsoft says a shared page can have up to 100 staff, and changes to some settings can take up to 10 minutes to apply.

Step 4: Or use a booking tool connected to Outlook

A booking tool makes sense when your meetings start on your website, when you want questions answered before the call, or when you need round robin across a sales team. Here's how it works in Claritia:

  1. Open Meetings and sign in with Microsoft. Microsoft 365 and Outlook.com accounts both work.
  2. Pick the calendars that count as busy and the one that gets new meetings. Free connects one calendar per seat, and paid plans connect six.
  3. Create a meeting type and set its location to Microsoft Teams.
  4. Set working hours, buffers, notice and daily caps.
  5. Share the booking page, put it in an email, or add it to the last page of a form.

Outlook tells Claritia as soon as your calendar changes, so a taken time never shows, and time you mark out of office is blocked too. See the Outlook integration for the details.

If you want new leads posted in a Teams channel as well, the Microsoft Teams integration does that on Starter and up.

Step 5: Find a time with a group using Scheduling Poll

When you need one time that works for five people, a booking page isn't the right tool. Outlook's Scheduling Poll is. In the new Outlook for Windows and Outlook on the web:

  1. Select New event and add your attendees.
  2. Pick the first day you'd consider, then select Scheduling poll.
  3. Review the suggested times, which are based on everyone's free and busy times. Delete or add options. Microsoft recommends 3 to 5, and allows up to 15.
  4. Add a title and agenda, and select Send.
  5. When the votes are in, open the event, choose the time with the most yes votes and select End poll with selected option.

In classic Outlook, you can turn on the Teams meeting option while you set up the poll. People outside your company vote on a secure web page. The new Outlook also places holds on everyone's calendars for each option until you pick one.

Step 6: Test your link

Before you share the link widely, book a meeting with yourself from a private browser window. Check three things:

  • The invite shows a Teams link, and it opens.
  • The time shows in the right time zone.
  • The slot you booked is gone from the page right after.

Common problems

  • No Teams link on bookings. Check that you're using a work or school account. In Shared Bookings, check that Add online meeting is on for the service and that Teams is the default meeting client.
  • People can book over busy times. In Shared Bookings, make sure calendar events affect availability for each staff member. Staff from outside your company can't share their free and busy times with Bookings.
  • Outside people can't open the page. The page may be set to require a Microsoft 365 account from your company. Turn that off to let anyone book.
  • You can't find Bookings at all. Your admin may have turned off Bookings or Personal Bookings. Ask them to check your mailbox policy.
  • Scheduling Poll is missing. Microsoft says it isn't supported for Outlook.com consumer accounts or for Gmail, Yahoo and other POP or IMAP accounts.
  • You need to take payment. Microsoft says Bookings has no payment support, so use a booking tool with Stripe instead.

Tools for scheduling Teams meetings

Claritia

Claritia is a forms, scheduling and follow-up app built for small B2B teams that want every lead from their website to reach the right rep and book a meeting before they leave the page. Microsoft Bookings

Microsoft Bookings is a scheduling app included with many Microsoft 365 business subscriptions, built for organizations that want customers or colleagues to book time without the back and forth. It offers personal booking pages for one person's availability and shared booking pages where teams define services, staff, and business hours. Bookings works with Outlook calendars, creates a Microsoft Teams link for online appointments, sends email and text reminders, and stores its data in Exchange Online. If your company already pays for an eligible Microsoft 365 plan, it's the natural first try, though it isn't sold on its own and can't take payments.

Calendly

Calendly is scheduling software for professionals and teams who want people to book meetings without trading emails. You connect your calendars, set hours, buffers, and daily limits, and share booking links for one-on-one, multi-host, or group meetings. It sends email and text reminders, offers routing forms that send website leads to the right teammate, and collects payments. Newer tools include an AI assistant that finds times over email and a notetaker that writes meeting recaps. It connects Outlook and Office 365 calendars, and its Teams video integration is on every plan. The free plan has one event type and no reminders.

Zoho Bookings

Zoho Bookings is appointment scheduling software for businesses of any size that want meetings to land on their calendar without back and forth. It offers one-on-one, group, collective, and resource-based event types, with workspaces for branches or departments, staff roles, and booking limits. Routing forms qualify leads before they book, workflows send email and SMS and update records, and it connects with Zoho CRM, Zoho Desk, and other Zoho apps. It syncs with Microsoft 365 and Outlook.com calendars and can add a Teams link to virtual meetings. Its free plan is for one user.

YouCanBookMe

YouCanBookMe is an online scheduling tool for small and midsize businesses that want a branded booking page tied to their calendars. It syncs with Google, Apple, Outlook, and Fastmail calendars, and offers booking forms with conditional questions, approval of requests before they are confirmed, password protected pages, and paid bookings through Stripe. It sends confirmations, reminders, and follow-ups by email and SMS, triggers webhooks, and reports on bookings, no-shows, and revenue. It connects to Microsoft Teams on its free plan, which allows 30 bookings a month.

Questions

Can people outside my company book a Teams meeting with me?

Yes. With Bookings with me, anyone with your link can book, even without a Microsoft account. Booking tools like Claritia also let anyone book from a link or form.

Does Microsoft Teams have a built-in scheduler?

Teams lets you schedule meetings with people you invite. To let others pick a time from a link, add the Bookings app to Teams or use a booking tool that adds Teams links.

Do I need a work account for Teams links?

In most tools, yes. Teams links come from a Microsoft 365 work or school account. In Claritia, personal Outlook.com accounts can still book, but without a Teams link.

Can I use Zoom instead of Teams with Outlook?

Yes, in booking tools that support both. In Claritia, connect Zoom and pick it as the meeting's location, and the Zoom link goes on the Outlook event.

Can a team share one Teams booking link?

Yes. Shared Bookings lets customers book with your staff. In Claritia, round robin on Pro shares bookings across the team and reads each person's Outlook calendar.
